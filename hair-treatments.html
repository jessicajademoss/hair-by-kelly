Hair health

The treatment your hair is actually asking for

← Back to the journal
28 Mar 2026Hair health

“Damaged” isn’t one problem — it’s several different ones that happen to look similar from a distance. The right treatment depends entirely on which one you actually have.

Dry, dull, but not breaking

If your hair feels rough to the touch and doesn’t reflect light the way it used to, but isn’t actually snapping, the issue is usually moisture, not structural damage. A deep-conditioning moisture treatment restores softness and shine without needing to address anything more intensive — and it’s often the fastest visible improvement of any treatment we offer.

Breaking, snapping, or won’t hold length

This is a different problem entirely — usually a loss of protein structure within the hair shaft itself, common after repeated colour, lightening or heat exposure. Moisture alone won’t fix this, and can occasionally make it feel worse. What’s needed is a bond-rebuilding or protein treatment that restores internal strength before anything else.

Moisture and protein solve two different problems. Using the wrong one doesn’t just fail to help — it can leave hair feeling worse than before.

A quick way to tell the difference at home

  • Stretch a strand of wet hair gently. If it stretches and springs back, that’s healthy elasticity.
  • If it stretches and stays stretched, or snaps with little resistance, that points to protein loss.
  • If it simply feels rough and looks flat, without breaking, that points to a moisture deficit.

Colour-treated hair needs both, in the right order

Lightened or frequently coloured hair often needs a structured approach — protein first to rebuild strength, followed by moisture to restore softness and shine. Doing this in a single unplanned treatment, or in the wrong order, tends to under-deliver on both fronts.

The scalp is part of this too

Healthy hair growth starts at the scalp, and a build-up of product or oil there can quietly undermine even the best treatment further down the length. A scalp-focused treatment or exfoliation, done occasionally alongside your usual visit, keeps the foundation right.

If you’re not sure which category your hair falls into, that’s exactly what a consultation is for. We’ll look at your hair’s actual condition, not just guess from how it feels, and build a treatment plan around what it genuinely needs.
