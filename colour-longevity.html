Colour care

How to make colour last longer between visits

← Back to the journal
14 Aug 2026Colour care

Colour rarely fades on its own. It fades through water, heat and the products layered on top of it — most of which is well within your control between visits.

Water is doing more damage than the sun

Hot water opens the hair cuticle, and every time it opens, colour molecules have a way out. Washing in lukewarm water, and finishing with a cool rinse, keeps the cuticle lying flat and holds pigment in rather than letting it wash down the drain. It sounds like a small habit, but it’s the single biggest factor we see in how quickly a colour looks tired.

Wash less often than you think you need to

Every wash is a small fade event, regardless of how gentle the shampoo is. Stretching washes to every second or third day — helped along with a good dry shampoo on the days in between — is one of the simplest ways to protect both vibrancy and the condition of the hair itself.

What to look for in a shampoo

  • Sulphate-free formulas, which cleanse without stripping colour
  • Cool or lukewarm application, every time
  • A colour-specific shampoo matched to your tone — blonde, brunette or red each fade differently

Heat styling: protect before you style, not after

A heat protectant applied before straightening or curling isn’t optional if you want colour to hold. Heat without protection accelerates both fading and the kind of dryness that makes colour look duller far sooner than it should.

The best colour maintenance happens quietly, at home, in the two minutes before you even pick up a straightener.

Book your top-up before the colour asks for it

Waiting until colour has visibly faded means starting from further behind each time. A toner refresh or gloss every four to six weeks keeps tone consistent, so the visit itself becomes lighter — a maintenance step rather than a rescue.

If your colour has been fading faster than it used to, it’s worth bringing that up at your next appointment — sometimes it’s the formula, and sometimes it’s simply the routine around it. Either way, it’s an easy thing to fix together.
