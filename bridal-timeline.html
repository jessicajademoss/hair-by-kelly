A bride’s timeline: when to book hair and makeup trials

← Back to the journal
19 May 2026Bridal

The best bridal hair rarely happens on the first try. It happens because there was enough time to test, adjust, and settle on something before it mattered.

6–9 months before: start the conversation

You don’t need a trial yet, but it’s worth coming in for a consultation — especially if colour or a cut change is part of the plan. This gives your hair enough runway to reach its healthiest state well ahead of the day, rather than rushing a big change in the final weeks.

4–6 months before: book the trial

This is the ideal window for your hair and makeup trial. It’s early enough to make changes without pressure, and close enough to your wedding that your hair’s length, colour and condition on trial day will closely match the real thing. Bring reference photos, but also bring your veil, headpiece or any accessories you plan to wear — they change how a style sits far more than people expect.

A trial isn’t a rehearsal for looking good. It’s the moment you find out what actually works — and what to leave behind.

What to bring to your trial

  • Your veil, headpiece or hair accessories, even if you’re not certain about them yet
  • Photos of styles you like — and just as usefully, ones you know you don’t
  • A photo of your dress neckline, which shapes how an updo or half-up style should sit

6–8 weeks before: any final colour or cut

If you’re colouring or cutting specifically for the wedding, this is the window to do it — close enough that it looks fresh on the day, far enough out that if anything needs adjusting, there’s time.

1–2 weeks before: a light finishing touch only

Reserve this window for small things only — a gloss, a trim, brow shaping. Nothing structural. The version of your hair your trial confirmed is the version you want to carry into the day.

The morning of

By this point, there should be nothing left to decide. The style is confirmed, the products are known, and the timing is planned around your ceremony, not squeezed in before it. That’s the entire point of doing the planning early — so the morning itself is simply enjoyed.
